Dear Mr Norman,

I am writing to apply for the research position advertised by the Museum of Natural History. I am a second-year postgraduate student studying for a PhD in marine biology, and I am particularly interested in endangered sea plants and the effects of pollution on marine ecosystems.

I would be grateful if you could provide some further information about the project. In particular, could you tell me which seas and oceans your team is studying? Could you please send me an application form and details of the selection process?

I look forward to hearing from you.

Yours sincerely,
Alex Morgan

Present tenses for future meaning

Exercise 1 · Build dialogues using the ideas. Use the present simple or present continuous. The first question is given as an example.

Quick reminder

Present continuous (am/is/are + -ing) expresses a future arrangement: We’re going to Italy in the summer.

Present simple is used in clauses after hope and depend on when referring to the future: I hope he gets the job. / It depends on when I finish.

Exercise 1 · Complete the dialogues

Present tense after depend and hope

A: Are you going out tonight?

B: It depends what time I finish work.

B: It depends how I’m feeling.

B: It depends if my girlfriend wants to.

I hope I pass my exam.

I hope I get paid tomorrow.

I hope he likes his birthday present.

After hope and depend (on), we normally use a present tense when the clause refers to the future. Avoid I hope I will pass in this exercise; use I hope I pass.
